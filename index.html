A MESSAGE FROM ANTHONY

Building a City That Works for Everyone

St. Catharines deserves leadership that focuses on the fundamentals while planning responsibly for the future.

Recent flooding has reminded us of the importance of our aging infrastructure. Roads, stormwater systems, sewers, and other critical infrastructure cannot simply be addressed when something fails. We need responsible long-term planning, clear priorities, and stronger efforts to secure available funding from other levels of government — so that necessary improvements do not fall entirely on local taxpayers.

We also need to restore accountability and accessible representation at City Hall. Residents deserve to know how decisions are made, how their tax dollars are being used, and that their concerns will be heard. As mayor, I will work to make City Hall more open, responsive, and connected to the people it serves.

And we must strengthen our local economy. St. Catharines should be a city where businesses want to invest, entrepreneurs can grow, and residents can find good jobs close to home. That means reducing unnecessary barriers, supporting our existing businesses, attracting responsible new investment, and working collaboratively with economic development partners across Niagara.

My commitment is straightforward: protect the infrastructure we depend on, restore trust and accountability, and build an economy that creates greater opportunity for everyone. — Anthony A. Adeoye

THE THREE PRIORITIES

What St. Catharines Needs Right Now.

01
Housing Affordability — A Crisis That Cannot Wait
25.7% new tenant premium · CMHC 2024 3.9% vacancy rate · CMHC 2025

New tenants in St. Catharines are paying 25.7% more than long-term tenants for the same units. With a rental vacancy rate of just 3.9% and only 1-2% of affordable units currently vacant, working families have almost no negotiating power.

New units are being permitted — but affordability for low and middle-income residents is still not improving fast enough. As mayor, I will use every tool available to close that gap.

02
Responsible Fiscal Management
10.5% property tax hike 2023 · City of St. Catharines

St. Catharines residents were hit with a 10.5% property tax increase in 2023. That burden is still being felt by homeowners and renters alike.

As mayor, I commit to fiscally responsible budgets — keeping increases at or below inflation — while working with council to identify efficiency savings and attract new investment that grows the city's tax base without adding burden to existing residents.

03
Community Safety — Closing the Perception Gap
77% feel crime rising · Numbeo 2024 CSI 54.7 — below national median · Statistics Canada

Niagara's Crime Severity Index improved to 54.7 in 2023 — below the national median. Yet 77% of St. Catharines residents still feel crime is increasing. That gap between data and lived experience matters.

As mayor, I will work with Niagara Regional Police and Regional Council to ensure St. Catharines receives its fair share of policing resources and that community prevention programs are accessible to all residents.
